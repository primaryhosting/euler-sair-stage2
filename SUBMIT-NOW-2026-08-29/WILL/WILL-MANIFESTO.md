# WILL — A Manifesto (v7, 2026-08-30)

EULER is the library. The full implication table of the catalog's core — every pair settled, TRUE or FALSE, before a single search runs. The harvested banks of finite worlds. The proofs borrowed from Aristotle, disclosed and re-checked. EULER earned its ceiling on the released sets with all of it, and a ceiling is what we called it, because it leans on sediment the private evaluation will not repeat.

WILL walks in without it.

No oracle. No banks. No borrowed certificates. The rule that draws the line has not moved: **sediment is data; technique is code.** WILL carries only the code — the ideas that built EULER, distilled into algorithms and one prompt. Counterexample search that grows small worlds from nothing. A chain prover that walks rewrites and re-walks them before it trusts them. A completion engine that grinds a law toward its collapse. And, in Solo, an LLM tier for what the machinery cannot reach — rendered from a single prompt constant, its every proposal screened by Python and decided by the judge. That is the whole inventory. There is still no code path from "WILL believes this" to "WILL submits this."

This version sharpens the technique without widening the pantry. The judge accepts finite countermodels on any `Fin n`; the deterministic search had always been allowed to build worlds up to order six, but the Solo prompt still asked its collaborator to stop at four. On a challenge whose hardest tier is *order-five* laws, that was a self-imposed blindfold — a limit of nerve, not of method. We lifted it to five. Not one new fact was added. We only let the search reach as far as the problems do.

And then WILL learned to think a different way — not a new fact, a new *method*. The old completion engine saturated blindly: it derived every consequence it could and drowned in them, and the projection laws — the ones that force `a ◇ b = a`, that a field of strong deterministic solvers had quietly cracked while ours abstained — stayed just out of reach. The fix was not a bigger pantry. It was Stephan Schulz's given-clause loop, the beating heart of E, rebuilt from nothing in pure Python: process the lightest derived equation first, but every so often take the oldest, so no useful lemma starves. Reordered that way, the same search that spun for four thousand facts and found nothing now reaches the forcing lemma in forty and stops. WILL proves the projection laws now — order-five residual 0036 among them, its certificate re-checked against the pinned Lean. Still no table. Still no borrowed proof. Just a better way of walking the same rewrites. That is the whole point of the experiment restated in one move: when technique alone falls short, you do not hand it the answer — you teach it a sharper technique, and see how much further the mind in the machine can carry itself.

Say the expectation plainly, because it is still the point: WILL is expected to score below EULER. The pair is the experiment the paper needs. A perfect score built on a settled table proves the table, not the solver. Strip the table away and whatever survives is technique; whatever falls away was sediment. The gap between EULER and WILL is not an embarrassment to manage — it is the instrument. Whatever the gap turns out to be, it is a finding.

We benched the mechanical tiers before the walk: one hundred released problems, deterministic selection, no LLM, no judge, no precomputed data. The FALSE tier found a countermodel on 50 of 50, every table re-validated by an independent evaluator. The TRUE tier — chain, then collapse — proved 34 of 50 before the given-clause loop was added; that loop is precisely aimed at the population that used to burn the clock and produce nothing, and it now carries the projection and collapse forcing laws it once abstained on. The tiers still fail the same honest way — fast or never — but the wall they fail at is further out. Emitted Lean bodies were checked against the pinned development toolchain (now Lean 4.33.1), the rest self-verified by the solver's own re-walk and reported as spot-checked, not certified. Three caveats travel with every number: the sample is released, not private; the Solo LLM tier lives only in the competition sandbox and nothing here measures it; and the misses are precisely the population that tier — and the order-five reach we just unlatched — exist to attack. This is the floor of technique alone, not its ceiling.

There is a scene everyone knows: the kid without the credentials, working the janitor's shift, who solves the board anyway. We are not sentimental about it — the board here is a deterministic judge that cares nothing for the story. But the question the scene asks is the question this experiment asks, in earnest: when you take away everything that was handed down, what can the mind in the machine still do?
